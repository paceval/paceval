# Optical thin-film metrology references (reviewed 15 November 2026)

The industrial research context is distinct from the synthetic paceval execution reported here.

1. [NIST, Filmetrics F40-UV reflectometer](https://www.nist.gov/laboratories/tools-instruments/nanofab-tool-filmetrics-f40-uv-reflectometer). Instrument descriptions and common film stacks; public context, not measured input data for WP 31.
2. [NIST, Woollam M-2000 DI spectroscopic ellipsometer](https://www.nist.gov/laboratories/tools-instruments/nanofab-tool-j-woollam-m-2000-di-spectroscopic-ellipsometer). Updated 4 March 2025; instrument capabilities including several hundred wavelengths, multiple angles and single-/multilayer fitting.
3. [J. A. Woollam, Ellipsometry FAQ](https://www.jawoollam.com/resources/ellipsometry-faq). Explains the distinction between reflectance/transmission intensity and ellipsometric phase and polarization measurements.
4. [S. J. Byrnes, Transfer-matrix method for optics of thin and thick multilayer films](https://github.com/sbyrnes321/tmm) and [related technical derivations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.02720). This repository is an established optical reference method; it is not linked or called by the C++ demonstrator.
5. [Rastgou et al., Comparison of thin-film thickness measurements using ellipsometry and reflectometry with uniform samples, *Metrologia* (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1681-7575/ae3964). Published 6 February 2026, emphasizing comparison and uncertainty in the metrology domain.
6. [Wilbrandt and Stenzel, Optimized Choice of Light Incidence Angles for Determination of Optical Constants in Absorbing Films, *Solids* (2026)](https://doi.org/10.3390/solids7030027). Published 27 May 2026, discusses ambiguities and optical parameter estimation.

**Research positioning:** WP 31 tests the real mathematical graph interface and its ability to orchestrate two transparent, versioned optical kernels. It does not claim a first demonstration of the Fresnel formula, an advantage over commercial reflectometers, or equivalence to full transfer-matrix ellipsometry.
