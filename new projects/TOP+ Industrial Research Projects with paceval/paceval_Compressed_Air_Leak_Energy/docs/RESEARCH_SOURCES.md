# Sources, industrial context and scientific boundaries

Research review dated 14 November 2026. The original source document **WP 19, Industrial Compressed Air Leak and Energy Optimization (13 November 2026)** proposed a leak-flow power law, simplified compression work, energy attribution and annual cost with a native reference. Its performance benchmarking suggestions were deliberately not included in the public output, in accordance with master prompt v3.1. The original document was a proposed experimental plan without measured outcomes.

1. [ISO 11011:2013, Compressed air — Energy efficiency — Assessment](https://www.iso.org/standard/46580.html). The formal 2013 standard remains current as of the review but a revision is under development. It covers supply, transmission and demand at the system level; our mathematical demonstrator is not a compliance assessment.
2. [DOE/ORNL MEASUR platform](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/ito/measur). Updated May 2025; includes compressed-air assessment methods and replacements for legacy AirMaster+ tools.
3. [ORNL MEASUR compressed-air assessments documentation](https://industrialresources.ornl.gov/measur/suite/docs/group__compressed__air__assessments). Includes compressor modeling and leakage-focused calculation capabilities; our study is narrower and does not replicate the entire software.
4. [DOE: Improving Compressed Air System Performance, Third Edition](https://betterbuildingssolutioncenter.energy.gov/better-plants/compressed-air). Discusses system baselining and leak testing; supports careful distinction between modeled loss and measured evidence.
5. Xiao, Yazdi and Thiede, [Ultrasonic-based leak detection in factories with spatial mapping](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.procir.2024.10.325), Procedia CIRP (2024). Documents practical ultrasonic measurements and localization, capabilities not provided by this mathematical kernel.
6. Hojjati and Radgen, [Comparison of Leak Localization and Quantification Methods for Compressed Air Systems Using Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis](https://doi.org/10.3390/en19071658), Energies (2026). Differentiates physical detection, quantification, uncertainty and operational tradeoffs.
7. Semitela and Completo, [Assessing leak detection and localization techniques for application in end-of-line leakage stations in the industrial sector](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psep.2025.108176), Process Safety and Environmental Protection (2026). Distinct application domain; acoustic methods and thermography can help with localization.
8. [MathWorks, Orifice (G)](https://www.mathworks.com/help/hydro/ref/orificeg.html). Provides a documented compressible gas orifice model with choked-flow behavior; actual real components require careful calibration.
9. [paceval official API reference](https://paceval.com/api/) and [Product Brief](https://paceval.com/product-brief/). Software and supported mathematical operator semantics are independently confirmed at runtime against the original SDK 4.25.

The industrial alternatives are broader system analysis, measurement and fault-localization technologies. The experiment checks **mathematical reuse and functional correctness**. It does not claim that paceval itself locates a leak, supplies calibrated measurements, replaces an industrial pneumatic simulator, or guarantees operational/financial improvements. The 2024–2026 research context should not be mistaken for experimental validation of this synthetic study.
